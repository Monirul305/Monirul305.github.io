Sharing a milestone announcement from the [Cybernetics Hi-Tech Solutions](https://cyberneticsbd.com) company page — we **delivered the CyberMaker Titan 550 Pro 3D printer** to **Dockyard and Engineering Works Ltd., Bangladesh Navy**.

This one is personal: the Titan 550 is the [large-format industrial CoreXY 3D printer]({{ '/projects/08_titan550/' | relative_url }}) I led the design and engineering on through two iterations at Cybernetics. We originally built it as an **in-house tool** for rapid prototyping of robot parts across the AGV, ROV, and drone platforms — and seeing it cross from internal R&D into a deployed unit at a national defence customer is genuinely satisfying.

### 🏗️ What was delivered

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Build volume | **510 × 510 × 550 mm** |
| Positioning accuracy | ±0.1 mm |
| Print speed | Up to 200 mm/s |
| Hotend temperature | Up to 300 °C |
| Bed temperature | Up to 120 °C |
| Bed levelling | Automatic via BLTouch probe |

The full v2 spec, including the ±0.02 mm repeatability, CoreXY kinematics, BigTreeTech + TMC2209 controller, and Marlin 2.0 firmware, lives on the [project page]({{ '/projects/08_titan550/' | relative_url }}).

### 🇧🇩 Why this one matters

Beyond the spec sheet, two things stand out about this delivery:

- **Designed, engineered, and validated entirely in-house** at Cybernetics' R&D lab in Dhaka — frame design through firmware, no white-labelling.
- **Deployed into a national defence customer** — Dockyard and Engineering Works Ltd. is a Bangladesh Navy entity, so the printer is now part of operational additive-manufacturing capability for the country's naval engineering pipeline.

As the company post put it — *innovation translated into national capability*. For a hardware team in Bangladesh, that handover is the part you remember.
