My teammate **Md. Tanvir Hassan**, an R&D Intern at [Cybernetics Hi-Tech Solutions](https://cyberneticsbd.com), shared a nice contribution he made to our **in-house PCB fabrication line** — a refined process for applying **UV solder mask** that's now part of our standard PCB workflow.

I'm one of the senior R&D engineers on the team Tanvir credits with the core fabrication pipeline. The UV-mask refinement, though, is squarely his contribution, and it's a meaningful upgrade to the part of the pipeline that gates how fine we can push trace geometry on boards we fabricate ourselves.

### 🧪 What changed

Tanvir's refinement targets the **solder-mask application stage** specifically — the layer that protects copper traces between pads, dictates how cleanly we can resolve small features, and ultimately determines whether we get clean isolation between adjacent traces or bridging defects.

The new method gives:

- **Higher-resolution masking** — required for scaling down to lower mil trace widths and clearances without bridging
- **More convenient application** — fewer manual touch-ups, more repeatable from board to board
- **Faster prototyping turnaround** — streamlining the masking stage compresses the loop from schematic-finalised to reliable PCB-in-hand

### 🛠️ Why in-house PCB fabrication matters for us

Cybernetics runs an in-house PCB line specifically because of the projects we build — AGVs, ROVs, drones, the [Titan 550 3D printer]({{ '/projects/08_titan550/' | relative_url }}), and a growing list of custom electromechanical platforms. Each of those needs custom control boards iterated quickly, often with through-hole and SMT mixed, and outsourcing every revision would kill iteration speed.

The senior R&D team built the core fabrication process (etching, drilling, hot-air levelling, the standard pipeline). What Tanvir's work does is push the **quality ceiling** of the masking stage — meaning the same in-house line can now reliably support tighter board layouts, closer to what we'd get from a commercial fab, without losing the in-day turnaround that makes it worth running ourselves.
