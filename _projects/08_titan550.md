Designed and manufactured two iterations of a large-volume industrial 3D printer that became Cybernetics' in-house workhorse — every Cybernetics platform you see elsewhere on this site (the EOD ROV chassis details, the manipulator carrier mock-ups, drone payload mounts, fixtures, jigs) goes through this machine at some point. V2 is the productised version with a direct-drive dual-gear extruder, a BigTreeTech / TMC2209 control stack, a 4.3″ touchscreen, and WiFi / Ethernet connectivity.

This is the industrial product I built at Cybernetics — distinct from my much earlier [undergraduate hobby printer]({{ '/projects/16_hobby_3d_printer/' | relative_url }}), which is a Cartesian-frame personal build laser-cut from acrylic. The Titan 550 is a from-scratch industrial design, not a remix.

### Why CoreXY at this Volume

A Cartesian printer at 510 mm of travel struggles: the X-gantry carries the Y-rail and the extruder, the Y-rail carries the X-rail; the moving mass per axis is high, ringing artefacts dominate at speed, and big steppers drift hot. **CoreXY decouples motion from mass** — both XY motors mount to the static frame, and only the toolhead moves on the gantry. That kept the moving mass low enough to hit the **200 mm/s** speed target without giving up the **±0.02 mm repeatability** spec.

### Frame, Motion & Bed

- **Frame:** welded steel base + 30-series aluminium extrusion uprights, sized for stiffness at the full 510 mm cantilever. Designed in SolidWorks against deflection and resonance targets — the frame's first mode sits well above the highest commanded print frequency so the motion system never excites it.
- **CoreXY motion:** GT2 belts running off two static-mounted NEMA 17 steppers; the toolhead's effective inertia stays roughly constant across the whole build area, which is what makes the repeatability spec hold at corners of the volume, not just dead-centre.
- **Z axis:** dual lead-screws, synchronously driven so the X-gantry stays parallel to the bed across the full 550 mm of vertical travel.
- **Bed:** removable PEI-coated build plate over a heated aluminium tooling plate, levelled by BLTouch with a 25-point probing grid before each print.

### Extruder & Hotend (v2 upgrade)

V1 ran a Bowden setup — fine for most rigid filaments at this scale, but the tube length and the metres of distance from the extruder gear to the melt zone made flexible filaments (TPU) and short retractions unreliable. **V2 replaced the Bowden with a direct-drive dual-gear extruder**, mounting the stepper directly on the toolhead. Retractions tightened up, TPU prints became consistent, and the spring-loaded dual-gear bite was strong enough to handle abrasive carbon-fibre-filled filaments without slipping.

The all-metal hotend tops out at **300 °C**, opening up engineering polymers (PETG, ASA, Nylon, PA-CF) for the industrial parts the machine actually has to produce.

### Electronics & Firmware

- **BigTreeTech 32-bit controller with TMC2209 stepper drivers** — TMC2209s give StealthChop (near-silent motion) and active current control, so the motors run cooler under the long industrial duty cycles this printer sees.
- **Marlin 2.0**, custom-configured for the CoreXY geometry, the dual-screw Z, the BLTouch probe grid, the thermal protection thresholds, and the connectivity stack.
- **4.3″ touchscreen** for on-machine job control plus full network access (WiFi / Ethernet) for slicing-to-print workflows.

### What It Prints

The Titan 550 is the fab loop for nearly every Cybernetics platform you see on this site:

- EOD ROV chassis covers, brackets, internal mounts.
- Manipulator end-effector adapters and tooling fixtures.
- Drone payload mounts and gimbal cradles.
- Test-fixture parts, cable-management blocks, gauge bodies.

Because the machine is in-house and reliable, the typical part-design loop is hours instead of days — the iteration speed shows up in every other project.
